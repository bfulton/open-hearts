Support

How to get help, and answers to what people ask.

Getting help

  • ♠
    Something is broken Open the game's menu and choose Tell us what's broken. It shows you everything it would send before you send it, and opens an issue on GitHub, where you can also report a problem directly. Issues are public, so put nothing private in one.
  • ♥
    Anything private A subscription, a refund, a privacy request, or a player to report: write or call us.

Questions

How do I play?

Open openhearts.game/play in any up-to-date browser and tap Play solo. The rules are short and are on the front page. If you tap a card you are not allowed to play, the game tells you which rule stopped you. Each game runs to fifty points, or a hundred if you choose the longer game; the lowest score wins.

Is it in the App Store or Google Play?

Not yet. Until it is, it plays in the browser on any phone, tablet or computer. Add the page to your home screen or bookmark it to find it again. Once the page has loaded, playing alone works without a connection.

Is it really free?

Playing alone against the bots is free and always will be: no ads, no account, nothing to buy. Only live play, against people, costs money.

How does live play work?

Live play is a subscription: $5.99 a year, paid up front. Tap Play live and you are seated in seconds with whoever is waiting, near your rating and your pace; seats nobody takes go to bots, which always say they are bots. Or tap Play friends for a table of your own and a link to send to three friends. Choose fifty points or a hundred, rated or unrated, before you start. The cards are dealt between the four devices, so no server, ours included, ever sees your hand.

Today live play is bought and played in the browser. The phone apps will offer it through the stores once they are there.

How do I subscribe?

In the browser, tap Subscribe under Play live on the game's first screen. Payment is taken on Stripe's own page, which asks for your email address and card. There is no account to make: the subscription belongs to the device you bought it on, and to any you add.

How do I cancel?

Live play keeps working until the end of the year you paid for, and the subscription renews each year until you cancel.

  • Bought on the web: open Live play in the game and choose Manage subscription, on any device on the subscription. It opens Stripe's billing page, where you can cancel, change the card or see receipts. Lost every device? Write to us or call, with the email address you paid with, and we will cancel it and confirm.
  • Bought in the App Store (once it is there): Settings, then your name, then Subscriptions.
  • Bought on Google Play (once it is there): the Play Store, your profile picture, then Payments & subscriptions.

Refunds are covered in the terms.

How do I add another device?

On a subscribed device, open the live-play page and tap Add a device. It shows a code. Point the other device's camera at it, and the link it reads brings that device in; or tap Send the link instead and open the link on the other device. A code works once, for ten minutes. Up to five devices can share a subscription, and any of them can drop another.

I lost my phone, or cleared my browser.

The subscription is kept on each device, not in an account. If another of your devices is still on it, use that one to add the new device, and drop the lost one. If none is, contact us with the email address you paid with and we will help.

Who are the bots?

Characters named after famous lovers — Romeo, Juliet, Cyrano and more — each with its own temperament, at nine strengths. At a live table the bots near your rating take the empty seats, and their ratings move with real results like everyone else's.

What happens to my data?

Playing alone, nothing leaves your device. There are no ads, no analytics and no account. Live play is public on purpose: your name, picture and results are published where anyone can read them, so pick a name and picture you are happy for strangers to see. You can play live with no picture, and hide anyone else's on your own device. The details are in the privacy policy.

Can I play with a screen reader, or bigger text?

Yes. Every card, seat, trick and score has a name, and who took a trick and whose turn it is are said aloud. In a browser, a screen reader first finds a button called Enable accessibility; press it once. We have played whole hands this way in a browser, but not yet with VoiceOver or TalkBack on a phone, so please tell us how it goes.

To make everything bigger, use the browser's zoom: Ctrl and + on a computer, ⌘ and + on a Mac. A tablet shows bigger cards than a phone.

Which languages does it speak?

English, español, português (Brasil), Deutsch and français, chosen from your device's language. To pick one yourself, add ?lang=es (or pt, de, fr, en) to the game's address. The translations are new; corrections are welcome.

How do I know the deal is fair?

At a live table every device shuffles in turn and proves it did, and one honest shuffle makes the deal fair; anyone can check the whole game afterwards. Alone, each deal comes from 256 bits of your device's secure random generator, and Copy record at the end of a game gives you everything needed to replay it. How it all works is written up in the project's documentation.

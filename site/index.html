What this is

Hearts is a trick-taking game for four players where every heart costs you a point, the queen of spades costs you thirteen, and the winner is whoever finishes with the fewest. It has been played for a century and there is still no version of it on a phone that is simply good.

Open Hearts is an attempt at one: fast, quiet, honest about its rules and its opponents, and open source from the first commit.

A deal nobody can rig

At a live table there is no dealer to trust. Every device at the table shuffles the deck in turn and proves it did so honestly, and one honest shuffle is enough for a fair deal. The cards are dealt so that each hand can be read by its player alone: no other player sees it, and no server sees it, ours included. After the hand, anyone can check the whole game against the deal. This is a technique called mental poker, built on a published protocol and on research code that has not had a security audit.

What it cannot stop is two players sharing their hands over the phone. No card game can.

Alone, every deal comes from 256 bits of your device's secure random generator, shuffled without bias, so every deal of the cards can come up and none follows from the one before. Each game alone is kept as a record you can copy and replay, trick by trick.

It works with a screen reader: every card, seat, trick and score has a name, and who took a trick is said aloud. We have played whole hands that way in a browser, through its accessibility tree. It has not yet been tried with VoiceOver or TalkBack on a phone, and we would like to hear from anyone who plays that way. In a browser, press the Enable accessibility button that a screen reader finds first on the page.

How Hearts works

  1. Four players, thirteen cards each.
  2. Pass three cards — left, then right, then across, then no pass at all.
  3. The two of clubs leads. Follow the suit that was led whenever you can.
  4. No hearts and no queen of spades on the first trick, unless you have nothing else.
  5. Hearts cannot be led until one has been played on some earlier trick.
  6. Every heart is one point; the queen of spades is thirteen. Points are bad.
  7. Take all twenty-six and you shoot the moon: everyone else takes them instead.
  8. The game ends after the hand in which someone reaches fifty points, or a hundred if you choose the longer game. Lowest score wins.

Where it is going

Today you can play alone, or live with people, in a browser. Next come the App Store and Google Play: playing alone first, then live play with the stores' own subscriptions. After that, trying the game with VoiceOver and TalkBack on real phones, and signing in with Apple or Google so a lost phone brings its subscription back with it.

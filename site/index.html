What this is

Hearts is a trick-taking game for four players where every heart costs you a point, the queen of spades costs you thirteen, and the winner is whoever finishes with the fewest. It has been played for a century and there is still no version of it on a phone that is simply good.

Open Hearts is an attempt at one: fast, quiet, honest about its rules, and open source from the first commit.

What makes it different

  • ♠
    It respects your attention No ads, no daily streaks, no chips to buy. You open it, you play a hand, you close it.
  • ♥
    It teaches while you play Tap a card you cannot play and it tells you which rule stopped you, instead of just refusing.
  • ♣
    The rules are the code A single, readable rules engine every player runs — no hidden server deciding what is legal.
  • ♦
    Yours to read and fork MIT licensed, developed in the open, and it will stay that way.

How Hearts works

  1. Four players, thirteen cards each.
  2. Pass three cards — left, then right, then across, then no pass at all.
  3. The two of clubs leads. Follow the suit that was led whenever you can.
  4. Hearts cannot be led until one has been played on some earlier trick.
  5. Every heart is one point; the queen of spades is thirteen. Points are bad.
  6. Take all twenty-six and you shoot the moon: everyone else takes them instead.
  7. First to a hundred ends it. Lowest score wins.

Where it is going

Today it is you against three bots, in a browser. Next comes playing with actual people — and the plan is to do it peer to peer, with no game server in the middle, so that there is nothing to pay for and nothing to shut down. Then the app stores.

Privacy

What Open Hearts does with what you give it, in plain words.

Last updated 1 October 2026

The short version

  • ♠
    Playing alone stays on your device Your name, your picture and your games against the bots are kept on the device you play on, and not sent anywhere.
  • ♥
    Live play is public, on purpose To play people, your name, your picture and your results go to relays — public servers, most of them run by other people — where anyone can read them and where they may be kept for good.
  • ♣
    No ads, no analytics, no accounts Nothing is sold, rented or used for advertising. There are no trackers in the app or on this site, and no account to make.
  • ♦
    Paying is Stripe's job On the web a subscription is bought through Stripe, which has your email address and card. We keep a note that your device is paid up. We never see your card number.

Who we are

Open Hearts is an open-source project. "We" are the people who maintain it, at github.com/bfulton/open-hearts. The code is public, so everything on this page can be checked against what the app actually does.

This page covers this site, the game at openhearts.game/play, and the Open Hearts apps for phones.

On your device

The app keeps these on the device you play on — in the browser's storage for this site, or in the app's own storage on a phone:

  • A key your device makes for itself. It is a long random number with a public half and a secret half. The public half is who you are at a live table. The secret half never leaves the device.
  • Your name and how you look: an icon and a colour, or a picture of your own. The picture is cut down to 64 by 64 pixels on your device, and only that small copy is kept. The photo you picked it from is not stored or sent.
  • Your last thirty games against the bots, which your profile counts up. They leave the device only if you tap Copy record.
  • How quickly you play, your game options, your licence if you subscribe, and the players whose pictures you chose to hide.

To remove all of it, clear this site's data in your browser, or delete the app. That also removes your key, so you come back as a new player, and it removes the licence a subscription gave that device.

Playing alone

A game against the bots is played entirely on your device. Nothing about it is sent anywhere.

In the phone apps, live play is not available yet, and until it is the apps make no network connections of their own. When live play comes to them it will work as described below, and this page will say so.

Opening the game on the web

  • Cloudflare hosts this site and the game, so it sees each request your browser makes: your IP address, your browser, and the address you asked for. We add no logging of our own.
  • Google's font servers, sometimes. The game carries its own font, but when it has to draw a character that font lacks — an emoji, an arrow, or a name written in a script such as Chinese or Arabic — the toolkit it is built with fetches the extra font from fonts.gstatic.com. When that happens, Google receives your IP address and the fact that you are visiting this site.
  • A licence check. Each time the game starts it sends your device's public key to our service to ask whether it holds a subscription. If it does not, nothing is kept.
  • Your profile page asks the relays for game results so it can show your rating. That connects your browser to them, so they see your IP address. Nothing about you is published by it.

Live play

What everyone can see

A live game is played between devices, through relays. A relay is a public server that passes messages between apps. Anything on a relay can be read by anyone who asks it, and some of it is kept. In live play:

  • Kept on relays and readable by anyone: your name, icon and colour, and your public key, each time you are seated at a table; your picture, if you chose one; and the result of every live game you play, rated or not — the table, each seat's key and final score, and who left. Ratings are worked out from these results by anyone who cares to, which is the point: nobody has to take our word for a rating.
  • Passed on but not kept: while you wait for a game, your name, rating, icon and colour and how many seconds you usually take over a card, every couple of seconds; and while the app is open, that your device is online or at a table. Relays forward these to whoever is listening at that moment and keep nothing — but anyone listening could record them.

A friends' table is private in one sense only: strangers cannot sit down at it. What it publishes is as public as any other table.

So choose a name you are happy for strangers to see, and do not use a photo you would not put on a public notice board. You can play live with no picture at all. On your own device you can hide anyone else's picture.

Where it goes

The game uses these relays today:

  • relay.openhearts.game and nostr.openhearts.game, ours. The first keeps nothing it is sent. The second is not running yet; when it is, this page will say what it keeps.
  • relay.damus.io, relay.primal.net and nos.lol, run by other people under their own policies. They may keep what is stored on them indefinitely, and anything signed can be copied from one relay to another by anyone.

We cannot delete anything from relays we do not run.

What only your table sees

The cards are dealt between the devices at the table so that each player's hand can be read by that player alone, and no server of ours ever sees a hand. Plays go directly from device to device wherever a direct connection can be made.

A direct connection means the devices at your table learn each other's IP addresses. So do the jurors: the apps of other players, drawn at random, that help settle a table and play the bots' seats. While your app is open and subscribed it serves as a juror at other people's tables too, so their devices learn your IP address.

To make those connections the app uses Cloudflare's connection services (STUN and TURN), which see your IP address. When two devices cannot reach each other directly, Cloudflare passes the traffic between them, encrypted so that it cannot read it.

Subscribing

Live play is a subscription. On the web it is bought through Stripe, on Stripe's own page: Stripe asks for your email address, your card and the billing details it needs, under its own privacy policy.

We give Stripe your device's public key so the payment can be matched to your device. In return our service keeps a short record: which key is on which subscription, when it was added, and the dates the subscription runs to. In Stripe we can see your email address, name, country, card brand, the last four digits of your card and your payments.

That means we could connect a subscriber's email address with their key, and so with the name and results it has published. We do not publish that connection, and we use it only to run your subscription.

If you add another device to your subscription, our service keeps a record of the one-use code you made and which devices made and used it. Any device on a subscription can see the others' keys and remove them.

What we don't do

  • No ads, and nothing sold or shared for advertising.
  • No analytics, no crash reports, no advertising identifiers.
  • No cookies from our pages. Stripe sets its own, on its own site, when you buy.
  • No location, no contacts, no microphone, no camera.
  • No accounts, and no email address held by us.
  • No chat.

How long things are kept

  • On your device: until you remove them.
  • On relays: whatever is passed on is not kept. Whatever is stored is kept as long as each relay's operator chooses, which may be for good.
  • Our service's subscription records: while the subscription runs, and afterwards until you ask us to delete them.
  • Stripe: as long as Stripe's policy and the law on payment records require.
  • Cloudflare and Google: under their own policies.

Your choices

  • Play alone, and nothing about you leaves your device.
  • Play live with a name that is not your real one, and with no picture.
  • Change your name or picture at any time. What was already published stays where it is.
  • Ask us for a copy of what our service keeps about your device, or ask us to delete it. We will also ask the relays we do not run to delete what your device published there, but we cannot make them.

Where data protection law gives you rights — to see, correct or delete what is held about you, to object, or to complain to your data protection authority — you have them here too.

Children

Open Hearts is not made for children under 13, and they should not play live, since it shows a name and picture to strangers. If you think a child has published something through it, tell us and we will do what we can to remove it.

Changes

If what the app does with data changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. Its history, and the code's, is on GitHub.

Contact

Open an issue at github.com/bfulton/open-hearts/issues. Issues are public, so put nothing private in one: say that you have a privacy request, and we will arrange a private way to deal with it.
